# Placement Content Playbook

This document explains where StudyBench should source learning material and questions from, how to convert those resources into original app content, and what quality bar the content must pass before we claim that it helps a student get placed in a target company.

The real product outcome is not "the student completed chapters." The outcome is:

> A student can clear the online assessment, perform in coding/technical rounds, communicate clearly in interviews, and get placed in the company they are targeting.

## 1. Core Principles

1. Outcome first: every lesson, quiz, mock, and challenge must map to a real hiring step.
2. Original content only: use resources to understand patterns and concepts, then write our own lessons, examples, questions, and explanations.
3. Official facts first: eligibility, dates, test modes, role names, and round structures must come from official company pages or official drive communications.
4. Practice beats reading: every concept must end with questions, timed drills, and error review.
5. Company-specific preparation: TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture, Cognizant, and Zoho should not feel like the same course with a different logo.
6. Honest readiness: PRI and placement probability must never inflate confidence. Weak areas should be visible and actionable.

## 2. Source Categories

Use sources in four tiers.

| Tier | Use for | Examples | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tier 1: Official | Eligibility, hiring process, assessment mode, role names, verified dates | Company careers pages, TCS iON NQT page, official campus mail, official job descriptions | Required for factual claims |
| Tier 2: Learning references | Concepts, topic coverage, teaching sequence | NPTEL, Infosys Springboard, GeeksforGeeks, HackerRank, LeetCode, CodeChef | Rewrite in our own words |
| Tier 3: Practice references | Common aptitude/coding/interview patterns | IndiaBIX, HackerRank practice, GFG practice, CodeChef courses | Use for coverage only; do not copy questions |
| Tier 4: Student reports | Difficulty trend, recent round experience, edge cases | TPO feedback, verified student reports, alumni interviews | Treat as signal, not truth |

Do not use leaked papers, paid course PDFs without permission, copied question banks, or screenshots from private assessment platforms.

## 4. Company-Specific Content Strategy

### TCS

Target outcome: clear NQT/campus assessment and interview for Ninja/Digital/Prime-style roles.

Content must include:

- Foundation numerical ability, verbal ability, and reasoning.
- Advanced aptitude and programming logic.
- Coding in C/C++/Java/Python with easy and medium problems.
- Timed section practice because section timing is a major failure point.
- Interview questions on projects, OOP, DBMS, CN, basic DSA, relocation, and shift flexibility.

Minimum app bank:

- 300 aptitude/reasoning/verbal questions.
- 80 programming logic MCQs.
- 80 coding problems, with 30 timed story-style problems.
- 10 full mocks matching the current verified pattern.

### Infosys

Target outcome: clear online/in-person assessment and technical + HR interview.

Content must include:

- Quantitative aptitude and logical reasoning.
- Pseudocode and programming logic.
- Coding fundamentals for Associate/System Engineer tracks.
- Stronger coding paths for Specialist Programmer/Digital Specialist roles.
- InfyTQ/Springboard-aligned foundational learning.

Minimum app bank:

- 250 aptitude/reasoning/verbal questions.
- 100 pseudocode/programming logic questions.
- 100 coding problems.
- 60 DBMS/OOP/Java/Python interview questions.

### Wipro

Target outcome: clear Elite-style assessment, essay/written communication, coding, and interview.

Content must include:

- Quant, logical, verbal.
- Essay writing with timed practice.
- Coding in allowed languages.
- HR questions around adaptability, learning, values, and service agreement.

Minimum app bank:

- 250 aptitude/reasoning/verbal questions.
- 50 essay prompts with evaluated sample structures.
- 80 coding problems.
- 8 full mocks with aptitude + essay + coding sections.

### Accenture

Target outcome: clear cognitive, technical, coding, communication, and interview stages.

Content must include:

- Cognitive aptitude: verbal, numerical, logical.
- Technical MCQs: networking, cloud basics, pseudocode, DBMS, OS, OOP.
- Coding problems.
- Communication assessment practice.
- Scenario and behavioral interview questions.

Minimum app bank:

- 250 cognitive questions.
- 150 technical MCQs.
- 80 coding problems.
- 100 communication drills.
- 8 full mocks.

### Cognizant

Target outcome: clear GenC-style assessment and interview.

Content must include:

- Aptitude and communication.
- Technical basics for role-specific assessments.
- Automata-fix/debugging style practice where relevant.
- Interview preparation for project, CS basics, and adaptability.

Minimum app bank:

- 250 aptitude/reasoning/verbal questions.
- 100 technical MCQs.
- 60 debugging/fix-code problems.
- 60 interview questions.

### Zoho

Target outcome: perform well in programming-heavy rounds and technical interviews.

Zoho needs a different track from service companies. Do not rely on aptitude-heavy preparation.

Content must include:

- Arrays, strings, recursion, loops, pattern printing, number logic.
- Medium-level DSA: hashing, sorting, two pointers, stack/queue, linked list, matrix.
- Machine-round style tasks: console applications, design small modules, debugging.
- Dry-run and complexity explanation practice.

Minimum app bank:

- 200 coding problems.
- 50 debugging problems.
- 30 machine-round mini tasks.
- 80 technical interview questions.
- 10 Zoho-style coding mocks.

## 6. Lesson Authoring Format

Every lesson should use this structure:

1. Why this matters in placement.
2. Core concept in simple language.
3. Formula/pattern/checklist.
4. Worked example.
5. Common mistake.
6. Fast solving tip.
7. Practice set.
8. Error review note.

Example lesson metadata:

```ts
{
  companyId: "tcs",
  sectionId: "quant",
  chapterId: "percentages",
  outcome: "Solve percentage change and ratio questions within 45 seconds",
  sourceIds: ["indiabix-aptitude", "studybench-original"],
  reviewedBy: ["SME_1", "SME_2"],
  lastReviewed: "2026-06-07"
}
```

## 7. Question Authoring Rules

Each question needs:

- Topic and subtopic.
- Difficulty: easy, medium, hard.
- Skill tested.
- Correct answer.
- Explanation.
- Estimated solving time.
- Source basis.
- Whether it is original, licensed, or reconstructed.
- Common wrong-answer reason.

Quality rule:

- Easy: tests one concept directly.
- Medium: combines two concepts or adds a trap.
- Hard: requires multi-step reasoning, edge cases, optimization, or time pressure.

Do not add questions just to increase count. A weak bank gives students fake confidence.

## 8. Daily Challenge Design

Daily challenges should build consistency, but they should also feed readiness.

Recommended format:

| Category | Questions | Time | Purpose |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| General | 8 | 10 min | mixed recall |
| Aptitude | 10 | 12 min | speed and accuracy |
| Coding | 1 coding + 3 MCQs | 20 min | implementation habit |
| Communication | 1 speaking/writing task | 5-10 min | interview confidence |

Daily challenge scoring should track:

- Accuracy.
- Time per question.
- Streak.
- Repeated weak topics.
- Whether the student reviewed mistakes.

## 9. Mock Test Design

The current 8-question mock is useful for demo, but not enough for placement readiness.

A real mock should have:

- Company-specific sections.
- Timer per section.
- No going back if the real test does not allow it.
- Section-wise cutoff risk.
- Full answer review.
- Weak topic mapping.
- Comparison against previous attempts.
- Action plan after mock.

Example TCS-style mock structure:

| Section | Questions | Time |
|---|---:|---:|
| Numerical Ability | 20 | 25 min |
| Verbal Ability | 25 | 25 min |
| Reasoning Ability | 20 | 25 min |
| Advanced Aptitude / Technical | 15 | 25-35 min |
| Coding | 2 | 60-90 min |

Only use this as a template. Verify the exact pattern each drive cycle.

## 10. Student Outcome Roadmap

The app should guide each student through four phases.

### Phase 1: Diagnose

Goal: know the student's true starting point.

App features:

- 30-minute diagnostic.
- Company preference.
- CGPA/backlog/branch/year check.
- Coding language selection.
- Communication self-rating.

Output:

- PRI per company.
- Weakest 5 topics.
- 30-day plan.

### Phase 2: Build Foundation

Goal: remove basic blockers.

App features:

- Guided lessons.
- Topic quizzes.
- Daily challenges.
- Mistake notebook.

Minimum readiness:

- 70%+ in quant/reasoning/verbal foundations.
- Can solve easy coding problems without watching solution.
- Can explain one project clearly.

### Phase 3: Company Sprint

Goal: prepare for one target company seriously.

App features:

- Company-specific mocks.
- PYQ-style sets.
- Timed practice.
- Coding drills.
- HR/technical interview practice.

Minimum readiness:

- 75%+ in two consecutive company mocks.
- Coding round minimum solved threshold based on company.
- Communication rubric score above safe threshold.

### Phase 4: Interview Conversion

Goal: convert assessment clearance into offer.

App features:

- Resume/project review checklist.
- Interview question bank.
- Mock interview scorecard.
- HR answer builder.
- Final-day checklist.

Minimum readiness:

- Can answer self-intro, project, strengths/weaknesses, why company.
- Can explain 2-3 coding solutions verbally.
- Can answer DBMS/OOP/OS/CN basics.

## 11. SME Review Workflow

Every content item should pass this flow:

1. Author drafts content.
2. SME reviews concept correctness.
3. Second reviewer checks clarity and difficulty.
4. Legal/IP check confirms no copied text or leaked paper.
5. Content is tagged with source IDs.
6. Test data is added to the app.
7. Student beta feedback is collected.
8. Weak/incorrect questions are revised.

Maintain this metadata:

```ts
{
  status: "draft" | "reviewed" | "live" | "needs_revision",
  author,
  reviewer,
  sourceIds,
  lastVerified,
  studentErrorRate,
  reportCount
}
```

## 12. Monthly Refresh Process

Do this every month during placement season:

- Verify official company pages.
- Ask TPOs/alumni for current assessment pattern.
- Review recent student reports, but mark them unofficial.
- Add 50-100 new questions to active company tracks.
- Retire stale questions.
- Recalibrate mock difficulty.
- Update last verified dates.

Do this immediately after a real drive:

- Collect anonymous student feedback.
- Record section difficulty.
- Record unexpected topics.
- Add reconstructed original practice questions.
- Update company-specific sprint plan.

## 13. What Not To Do

Avoid these mistakes:

- Do not market the app as a placement guarantee.
- Do not copy PYQs from Telegram, PDFs, or paid courses.
- Do not use one generic curriculum for all companies.
- Do not make mocks too easy to improve user confidence.
- Do not ignore coding implementation practice.
- Do not skip communication assessment practice.
- Do not let stale eligibility data remain without a warning.

## 15. Success Metrics

Track these instead of only XP:

| Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Diagnostic to mock improvement | shows real learning |
| Two-mock consistency | avoids one lucky score |
| Coding test case pass rate | predicts coding round readiness |
| Weak-topic closure rate | shows targeted improvement |
| Communication rubric score | predicts HR/communication rounds |
| Interview mock pass rate | predicts final conversion |
| Self-reported assessment cleared | real intermediate outcome |
| Self-reported offer received | final outcome |

## 16. Final Standard

A track is ready to launch only when a serious student can use it as the primary preparation path for that company.

That means:

- They know the current verified process.
- They have enough practice volume.
- They can simulate the real test.
- They can review mistakes.
- They can prepare for interview conversion.
- They receive honest warnings when they are not ready.

If any of these are missing, call the track "starter" or "beta", not placement-ready.
