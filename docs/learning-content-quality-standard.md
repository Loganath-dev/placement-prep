# StudyBench learning-content quality standard

This standard applies to Aptitude, Logical Reasoning, Verbal, Programming Languages, CS Core, DSA, SQL/DBMS, Company Tracks, PYQs and Mock Tests.

## Teaching-card requirements

Every lesson should teach one small idea per screen in this order where relevant:

1. Learning objective and prerequisite.
2. Plain-language concept explanation.
3. Formula, rule, syntax or mental model.
4. Worked example with each step explained.
5. Placement-style application.
6. Common mistake or trap.
7. Short recap and a transition to practice.

Keep the card answer concise enough to understand without an inner scroll area. Split long prose at sentence/paragraph boundaries. Do not split a code example arbitrarily; keep code intact and explain it in a separate card.

## Lesson checkpoint requirements

- A lesson is mastered only after the student completes its learning cards and scores at least 60% on its lesson checkpoint.
- The next lesson remains locked until the previous lesson is passed.
- A failed attempt must show the correct answer and a useful explanation for each missed question, allow a return to the relevant learning cards, and permit a retry.
- A chapter assessment is a separate checkpoint and remains at 70%; it unlocks the next chapter only after it is passed.
- Daily and weekly reviews may include only lessons the student has already passed.

## Question-bank requirements

The target is approximately 20 questions per lesson, with a balanced mix of easy, medium and hard questions. Every question should have:
- a single unambiguous prompt;
- a verified answer key;
- a step-by-step explanation (including why a tempting distractor is wrong when useful);
- a topic and difficulty that match the lesson;
- an explicit lesson mapping (`lessonId`) or reviewed concept tags (`topicTags`) wherever possible;
- source/provenance metadata and a recorded content-review decision.

Do not add unrelated filler to reach 20. When the reviewed bank is short, show the actual count and record a content gap for authoring. Generated practice must remain visibly distinguished from trainer-reviewed questions.

## Source and licensing policy

Use open educational resources as references for concepts and structure, then write original StudyBench explanations, examples and placement questions. Do not copy textbook paragraphs, diagrams or question sets wholesale. Record source title, author/organization, URL, version/edition, license, access date and the specific concepts informed.

Potential reference libraries (verify the license on the exact item before use):
- OpenStax Computer Science: https://openstax.org/subjects/computer-science
- OpenStax Mathematics: https://openstax.org/subjects/math
- OpenDSA open textbooks and interactive DSA materials: https://opendsa.org/home/books
- University of Hawaiʻi English Composition OER: https://pressbooks.oer.hawaii.edu/englishcomposition/
- BCcampus Open Education: https://open.bccampus.ca/

For a paid product, do not adapt or redistribute resources carrying NonCommercial or NoDerivatives restrictions unless written permission is obtained. Prefer commercially compatible licenses such as CC BY, follow attribution requirements, and keep a record of the exact license/version used. Publicly available does not mean public domain.

## Academic quality gates before publishing

A second reviewer (not the author) should independently solve the question and check:
1. answer key;
2. explanation and worked steps;
3. source and license;
4. topic and difficulty;
5. duplicate/near-duplicate status.

Do not describe a generated question as trainer-authored or a PYQ unless its origin and year have been verified.
